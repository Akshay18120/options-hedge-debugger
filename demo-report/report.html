How to use the findings

Inspect whether losses are dominated by nonlinear option exposure, volatility repricing or repeated hedging costs. Change one assumption at a time and rerun the identical scenario grid. Treat the fixed threshold as a baseline, not a calibrated optimum. Adding a hedge instrument requires a new model and separate evaluation.

Model boundaries

  • European options on one underlying, marked with Black–Scholes–Merton. No early exercise, stochastic rates, volatility smile or option bid/ask valuation.
  • The option book is held to the horizon. Only underlying shares trade. Their opening and final liquidation costs are included. Fractional shares are permitted.
  • Flat borrowing/lending rate; continuous dividend yield paid approximately once per day. Dividends are charged for short shares.
  • Daily decision points. Overnight jumps occur before the next rebalance. The initial hedge can trade; outages block days 1 through the specified duration. Final liquidation is assumed available.
  • Underlying execution is at spot plus proportional spread and per-share fees. No market impact, partial fills or queue model. Outages represent unavailable hedge opportunities, not calibrated exchange latency.
  • IV is floored at 1%. The roundtrip price path returns to its initial spot; volatility can still change. A quote-bounded hedge size can leave residual exposure.
  • No historical or live performance claim. Scenario search provides neither probabilities nor confidence levels. Results do not establish an optimal or safe hedge.

Reproduce this report
